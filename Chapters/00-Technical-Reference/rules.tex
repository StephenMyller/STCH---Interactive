\subsection{Gentzen ND}


\nbgpara{1}{Derivations}
A derivation is a finite rooted tree of formula occurrences constructed in accordance with the inference rules stated below. Its lowermost formula occurrence is its conclusion. Each uppermost formula occurrence is either an assumption or the conclusion of a rule with no premises.

Since derivations are trees of formula occurrences, distinct nodes may contain the same formula while occupying different positions in the derivation and depending on different assumptions.

\nbgpara{2}{Assumptions and Dependency}
An assumption occurrence may be marked with an assumption label. The assumption labels are natural numbers. We use \(n,m,\ldots\) as metavariables ranging over assumption labels. Thus, if \(n\) is an assumption label, then \(n+1\) is also an assumption label. An assumption occurrence marked with the label \(n\) is written as follows:

\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(n\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\varphi\)}
\end{prooftree}

Assumption labels are metalinguistic bookkeeping marks. They are not symbols of the language of NBG and are not parts of the formulas to which they are attached.

Within a derivation, each assumption label identifies a single discharge class. All assumption occurrences carrying the same label are occurrences of the same formula, and a label is associated with at most one application of a discharge rule. If a label is associated with such an application, every occurrence carrying that label lies within the premise subderivation designated by the rule. The same labels may be reused in separate derivations.

When an application of a discharge rule carries the label \(n\), it discharges every still-undischarged assumption occurrence marked with \(n\) in the premise subderivation designated by that rule. A single application may therefore discharge several occurrences of the same assumption.

For \(\to\)-I, \(\neg\)-I, and CR, the designated premise subderivation is the sole premise subderivation of the rule. In an application of \(\lor\)-E, each label applies only to its corresponding minor premise subderivation. In an application of \(\exists\)-E, the label applies only to the minor premise subderivation beginning with the assumed instance of the existential formula.

The dependency of formula occurrences on assumptions is defined recursively. For each formula occurrence \(\theta\) in a derivation, let \(\Dep(\theta)\) be its dependency set.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item If \(\theta\) is an assumption occurrence, then
    \[
    \Dep(\theta)=\{\theta\}.
    \]

    \item If \(\theta\) is the conclusion of a rule with no premises, then
    \[
    \Dep(\theta)=\varnothing.
    \]

    \item Suppose that \(\theta\) is the conclusion of an application of a rule whose premise occurrences are
    \[
    \theta_1,\ldots,\theta_k.
    \]
    Let \(\Delta\) be the set of assumption occurrences discharged by that application. Then
    \[
    \Dep(\theta)
    =
    \bigl(
    \Dep(\theta_1)\cup\cdots\cup\Dep(\theta_k)
    \bigr)
    \setminus\Delta.
    \]
\end{enumerate}

A formula occurrence \(\theta\) depends on precisely the assumption occurrences belonging to \(\Dep(\theta)\). The undischarged assumptions of a derivation are the assumption occurrences belonging to the dependency set of its conclusion.

\nbgpara{3}{Turnstile}
Let \(\Gamma\) be a collection of formulas and let \(\varphi\) be a formula. We write
\[
\Gamma\vdash\varphi
\]
iff there exists a derivation of \(\varphi\) such that the formula occurring at every undischarged assumption occurrence is a member of \(\Gamma\). This is read as ``\(\varphi\) is derivable from \(\Gamma\).'' Thus, the symbol \(\vdash\) is a symbol of the metalanguage, not of the language of NBG.

The definition does not require every member of \(\Gamma\) to occur as an undischarged assumption. Consequently, if
\[
\Gamma\vdash\varphi
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\Gamma\subseteq\Delta,
\]
then
\[
\Delta\vdash\varphi.
\]
Weakening is therefore built into the definition of derivability.

The collection \(\Gamma\) also does not record the multiplicity of assumption occurrences. Several undischarged occurrences of the same formula are represented by a single member of \(\Gamma\), although they remain distinct occurrences within the derivation itself.

\nbgpara{4}{Proofs}
A derivation with no undischarged assumptions is called a proof. Its conclusion is called a theorem of the deductive system in which the derivation is carried out.

When the derivation has no undischarged assumptions, we write
\[
\vdash\varphi
\]
instead of
\[
\varnothing\vdash\varphi.
\]
Thus, \(\vdash\varphi\) means that there exists a proof of \(\varphi\), or equivalently that \(\varphi\) is a theorem of the deductive system.

\nbgpara{12}{Universal Quantification}

\nbgparar{$\bm{\forall}$-I} \quad Universal quantification is governed by the following introduction rule: \vspace{0.3cm}

\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{\(\varphi[\eta/\xi]\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\forall\)-I}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\forall\xi\,\varphi\)}
\end{prooftree} \vspace{0.3cm}

The variable $\eta$ is called the eigenvariable of the application. Intuitively, it is treated as arbitrary: no undischarged assumption on which the premise depends may contain $\eta$ free.

This is made precise by the following side conditions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \(\eta\) is free for \(\xi\) in \(\varphi\);

    \item \(\eta\) does not occur free in \(\varphi\); and

    \item \(\eta\) does not occur free in any undischarged assumption on which the premise occurrence \(\varphi[\eta/\xi]\) depends.
\end{enumerate}

\nbgparar{$\bm{\forall}$-E} \quad Universal quantification is governed by the following elimination rule: \vspace{0.3cm}

\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{\(\forall\xi\,\varphi\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\forall\)-E}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\varphi[\eta/\xi]\)}
\end{prooftree} \vspace{0.3cm}

The rule is permitted only if \(\eta\) is free for \(\xi\) in \(\varphi\).

\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{\(\varphi[\eta/\xi]\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\exists\)-I}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\exists\xi\,\varphi\)}
\end{prooftree} \vspace{0.3cm}

The rule is permitted only if \(\eta\) is free for \(\xi\) in \(\varphi\).

\vspace{0.3cm}

\nbgparar{$\bm{\exists}$-E} \quad Existential quantification is governed by the following elimination rule:

\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{\(\exists\xi\,\varphi\)}
%
    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(n\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\varphi[\eta/\xi]\)}
    \noLine
    \UnaryInfC{\(\vdots\)}
    \noLine
    \UnaryInfC{\(\psi\)}
%
    \RightLabel{\(\exists\)-E, \(n\)}
    \BinaryInfC{\(\psi\)}
\end{prooftree} \vspace{0.3cm}

The right-hand premise subderivation ending with the displayed occurrence of $\psi$ is called the minor premise subderivation. It contains the assumption occurrences $\varphi[\eta/\xi]$ marked with $n$, which are discharged by the application. The application of \(\exists\)-E discharges the assumption occurrences marked with \(n\) only within this subderivation.

The variable \(\eta\) is called the eigenvariable of the application. Its role is to represent an arbitrary witness to the existential statement about which no other relevant assumption has been made.

This is made precise by the following side conditions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \(\eta\) is free for \(\xi\) in \(\varphi\);

    \item \(\eta\) does not occur free in \(\exists\xi\,\varphi\);

    \item \(\eta\) does not occur free in \(\psi\); and

    \item \(\eta\) does not occur free in any undischarged assumption on which the minor premise derivation of \(\psi\) depends, other than the assumption occurrences discharged by the application.
\end{enumerate}

\nbgpara{14}{Uniqueness and Unique Existence}

\begin{definition}[Uniqueness]
\label{def:uniqueness}
We write $\Unique x\,\varphi(x)$ to say that \emph{at most one}
object satisfies $\varphi$. This is an abbreviation for
\[
\Unique x\,\varphi(x)
\quad\defequiv\quad
\forall y\forall z\bigl(
\varphi(y)\to(\varphi(z)\to y=z)
\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Unique Existence]\label{def:unique-existence}
We write $\exists!x\,\varphi(x)$ to say that \emph{exactly one}
object satisfies $\varphi$. This abbreviates existence together
with at-most-one uniqueness:
\[
\exists!x\,\varphi(x)
\quad\defequiv\quad
\exists x\,\varphi(x) \ \land \ \Unique x\,\varphi(x).
\]
\end{definition}

Here $y$ and $z$ are distinct fresh variables, and $\varphi(y)$ and
$\varphi(z)$ denote the corresponding substitutions for $x$.
The notation $\Unique$ thus expresses \emph{uniqueness} alone,
whereas $\exists!$ expresses \emph{unique existence}.

Neither abbreviation introduces a primitive logical rule.
We first establish an introduction rule for at-most-one uniqueness,
and then introduction and elimination rules for unique existence.

\begin{derivedrule}[Uniqueness Introduction]
\label{rule:uniqueness-intro}
Suppose $a$ and $b$ are distinct variables. If a derivation of $a=b$
depends on assumptions $\varphi(a)$ and $\varphi(b)$, marked with
$n$ and $m$ respectively, then both assumption classes may be
discharged to conclude at-most-one uniqueness:
\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(n\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\varphi(a)\)}
    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(m\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\varphi(b)\)}
    \noLine
    \BinaryInfC{\(\vdots\)}
    \noLine
    \UnaryInfC{\(a=b\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\Unique\)-I, \(n,m\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\Unique x\,\varphi(x)\)}
\end{prooftree}
The application discharges every still-undischarged occurrence
marked with $n$ or $m$ in its premise subderivation. The variables
$a$ and $b$ must be distinct, free for $x$ in $\varphi(x)$, and
absent free from $\varphi(x)$ and from all other undischarged
assumptions on which the premise depends. The labels $n$ and $m$
are distinct and do not label assumptions discharged earlier.
\end{derivedrule}

To justify the rule, let $\mathcal D$ be the given derivation
of $a=b$ from the two marked assumption classes. First apply
$\to$-I to discharge the occurrences marked $m$, and then apply
$\to$-I to discharge those marked $n$. The result has the form
$\varphi(a)\to(\varphi(b)\to a=b)$. Next introduce the two
universal quantifiers and invoke the definition of uniqueness.

% proof-explorer-expansion: rule:uniqueness-intro; kind=unique; mode=atmost-intro
\begin{formalderivation}
\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(n\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\varphi(a)\)}
    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(m\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\varphi(b)\)}
    \noLine
    \BinaryInfC{\(\vdots\)}
    \noLine
    \UnaryInfC{\(a=b\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\to\)-I, \(m\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\varphi(b)\to a=b\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\to\)-I, \(n\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\varphi(a)\to(\varphi(b)\to a=b)\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\forall\)-I}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\forall z\,(\varphi(a)\to(\varphi(z)\to a=z))\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\forall\)-I}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\forall y\forall z\,(\varphi(y)\to(\varphi(z)\to y=z))\)}
    \RightLabel{D\ref{def:uniqueness}}
    \dashedLine
    \UnaryInfC{\(\Unique x\,\varphi(x)\)}
\end{prooftree}
\end{formalderivation}

The two $\to$-I applications discharge exactly the two specified
assumption classes.
The $\forall$-I applications are legitimate because $a$ and $b$
occur free in no remaining undischarged assumption and are absent
free from $\varphi(x)$. Consequently, the last formula is
$\Unique x\,\varphi(x)$ by definition, so $\Unique$-I is admissible.

\begin{derivedrule}[Unique Existence Introduction]
\label{rule:unique-existence-intro}
\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{\(\exists x\,\varphi(x)\)}
    \AxiomC{\(\Unique x\,\varphi(x)\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\exists!\)-I}
    \BinaryInfC{\(\exists!x\,\varphi(x)\)}
\end{prooftree}
\end{derivedrule}

% proof-explorer-expansion: rule:unique-existence-intro; kind=unique; mode=intro
\begin{formalderivation}
\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{\(\exists x\,\varphi(x)\)}
    \AxiomC{\(\Unique x\,\varphi(x)\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\land\)-I}
    \BinaryInfC{\((\exists x\,\varphi(x))\land\Unique x\,\varphi(x)\)}
    \RightLabel{D\ref{def:unique-existence}}
    \dashedLine
    \UnaryInfC{\(\exists!x\,\varphi(x)\)}
\end{prooftree}
\end{formalderivation}

\begin{derivedrule}[Unique Existence Elimination]
\label{rule:unique-existence-elim}
From unique existence we may infer existence or at-most-one
uniqueness independently.
\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{\(\exists!x\,\varphi(x)\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\exists!\)-E}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\exists x\,\varphi(x)\)}
\end{prooftree}
\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{\(\exists!x\,\varphi(x)\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\exists!\)-E}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\Unique x\,\varphi(x)\)}
\end{prooftree}
\end{derivedrule}

Both forms follow by definition and Conjunction Elimination.

% proof-explorer-expansion: rule:unique-existence-elim; kind=unique; mode=elim
\begin{formalderivation}
\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{\(\exists!x\,\varphi(x)\)}
    \RightLabel{D\ref{def:unique-existence}}
    \dashedLine
    \UnaryInfC{\((\exists x\,\varphi(x))\land\Unique x\,\varphi(x)\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\land\)-E}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\exists x\,\varphi(x)\)}
\end{prooftree}

\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{\(\exists!x\,\varphi(x)\)}
    \RightLabel{D\ref{def:unique-existence}}
    \dashedLine
    \UnaryInfC{\((\exists x\,\varphi(x))\land\Unique x\,\varphi(x)\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\land\)-E}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\Unique x\,\varphi(x)\)}
\end{prooftree}
\end{formalderivation}

\nbgpara{16}{Compressed Derivation Displays}
In later derivations, we shall occasionally suppress a finite sequence of routine applications of the same inference rule. If \(R\) is an inference rule, a displayed application labelled

$$
R^{*}
$$

indicates that the displayed premise occurrences and conclusion are connected by a finite nonempty derivation segment whose inference applications are all applications of \(R\). Intermediate formula occurrences within that segment are omitted from the display.

Thus, for example, a step of the form

\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{\(\varphi\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\forall\)-I\(^{*}\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\forall\xi_1\cdots\forall\xi_k\,\varphi\)}
\end{prooftree}

abbreviates the corresponding succession of applications of \(\forall\)-I, with the quantifiers introduced one at a time in the order required to obtain the displayed conclusion.

Similarly, a displayed application of \(\land\)-I\(^{*}\), \(\land\)-E\(^{*}\), or another rule may abbreviate the corresponding finite derivation segment.

This convention concerns only the display of derivations and introduces no new inference rules. Every suppressed application must be a legitimate application of the ordinary rule and must separately satisfy all side conditions governing that rule.




\nbgpara{17}{Basic Derived Rules}

We establish two elementary derived rules that will be used
in later derivations. Each is admissible in the deductive
system already defined and introduces no additional
inference principle.

\nbgparar{Negation Congruence} \quad
From a biconditional, we may infer the biconditional
of the corresponding negations:

\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{\(\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi\)}
    \RightLabel{NC}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\neg\varphi\leftrightarrow\neg\psi\)}
\end{prooftree}

To justify this rule, we derive
\[
\vdash
(\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi)
\to
(\neg\varphi\leftrightarrow\neg\psi).
\]

\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(1\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi\)}
    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(2\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\psi\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\leftrightarrow\)-E}
    \BinaryInfC{\(\varphi\)}
    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(3\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\neg\varphi\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\neg\)-E}
    \BinaryInfC{\(\bot\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\neg\)-I, \(2\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\neg\psi\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\to\)-I, \(3\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\neg\varphi\to\neg\psi\)}
%
    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(1\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi\)}
    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(4\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\varphi\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\leftrightarrow\)-E}
    \BinaryInfC{\(\psi\)}
    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(5\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\neg\psi\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\neg\)-E}
    \BinaryInfC{\(\bot\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\neg\)-I, \(4\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\neg\varphi\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\to\)-I, \(5\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\neg\psi\to\neg\varphi\)}
%
    \RightLabel{\(\leftrightarrow\)-I}
    \BinaryInfC{\(\neg\varphi\leftrightarrow\neg\psi\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\to\)-I, \(1\)}
    \UnaryInfC{
        \((\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi)
        \to
        (\neg\varphi\leftrightarrow\neg\psi)\)
    }
\end{prooftree}

Thus, an application of Negation Congruence abbreviates
an application of the displayed theorem followed by
\(\to\)-E.

\vspace{0.4cm}

\nbgparar{Symmetry of Identity} \quad
Identity may be reversed:

\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{\(\xi=\eta\)}
    \RightLabel{\(=\)-Sym}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\eta=\xi\)}
\end{prooftree}

To justify this rule, we derive
\[
\vdash
\xi=\eta\to\eta=\xi.
\]

\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(1\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\xi=\eta\)}
    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(=\)-I}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\xi=\xi\)}
    \RightLabel{\(=\)-E}
    \BinaryInfC{\(\eta=\xi\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\to\)-I, \(1\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\xi=\eta\to\eta=\xi\)}
\end{prooftree}

The application of \(=\)-E uses the formula
\[
\zeta=\xi,
\]
substituting \(\xi\) and \(\eta\), respectively, for
\(\zeta\). Both substitutions satisfy the side conditions
of the primitive rule. Thus, an application of \(=\)-Sym likewise abbreviates
an application of the displayed theorem followed by
\(\to\)-E.

 For the sake of economy, throughout our derivations, we allow the two sides
of an equality to be exchanged without explicitly
    invoking the symmetry of identity. Thus, whenever
    $a=b$ has been established, we may also use $b=a$.
    Such steps are understood to abbreviate an
    application of the derived rule $=$-Sym.


\vspace{0.4cm}

\nbgparar{Disjunctive Syllogism} \quad
From a disjunction and the negation of one
of its disjuncts, we may infer the other:

\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{\(\varphi\lor\psi\)}
    \AxiomC{\(\neg\varphi\)}
    \RightLabel{DS}
    \BinaryInfC{\(\psi\)}
\end{prooftree}

To justify this rule, we derive
\[
\vdash
(\varphi\lor\psi)\to(\neg\varphi\to\psi).
\]

\begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(1\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\varphi\lor\psi\)}

    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(3\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\varphi\)}

    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(2\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\neg\varphi\)}

    \RightLabel{\(\neg\)-E}
    \BinaryInfC{\(\bot\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\bot\)-E}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\psi\)}

    \AxiomC{}
    \RightLabel{\(4\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\psi\)}

    \RightLabel{\(\lor\)-E, \(3,4\)}
    \TrinaryInfC{\(\psi\)}

    \RightLabel{\(\to\)-I, \(2\)}
    \UnaryInfC{\(\neg\varphi\to\psi\)}
    \RightLabel{\(\to\)-I, \(1\)}
    \UnaryInfC{
        \((\varphi\lor\psi)\to
        (\neg\varphi\to\psi)\)
    }
\end{prooftree}

Thus, an application of DS abbreviates
an application of the displayed theorem
followed by two applications of $\to$-E.
The rule is also admissible with the
disjuncts exchanged.



None of these derived rules adds deductive
power. All may henceforth be used
as single steps in derivation displays.
