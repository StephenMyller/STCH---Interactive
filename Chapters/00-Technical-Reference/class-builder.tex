\subsection{Class Term Extension}

\subsubsection{Class-Term Syntax and Contextual Elimination}

\nbgpara{1}{General Class Terms}

The primitive language of NBG contains no terms other than variables.
We nevertheless wish to use expressions such as
\[
\{\xi\mid\varphi\}
\]
as though they were terms denoting classes. Before establishing that
this practice is legitimate, we must specify the expressions involved
and explain how formulas containing them are to be understood in the
original language.

\begin{definition}[General Class Terms]
\label{def:general-class-terms}
Every variable is an \emph{extended term}.

If \(\varphi\) is a predicative extended formula and \(\xi\) is a
variable, then
\[
\{\xi\mid\varphi\}
\]
is a \emph{general class term}, provided that all class terms occurring
in \(\varphi\) have already been admitted at an earlier stage of the
construction. Every general class term is an extended term.

If \(S\) and \(T\) are extended terms, then
\[
S\in T
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
S=T
\]
are atomic extended formulas.
\end{definition}

The ordinary logical formation rules extend to these atomic formulas.
Previously admitted defined notation may also be used in accordance
with its formation clauses. In particular, the introduction of class
terms does not by itself extend every previously defined predicate to
arbitrary extended-term arguments. Such extensions will be justified
separately where required.

In \(\{\xi\mid\varphi\}\), the variable \(\xi\) binds precisely its
free occurrences in \(\varphi\). Thus the free variables of the class
term are the free variables of \(\varphi\) other than \(\xi\).
Alphabetic variation and substitution of variables for variables are
extended recursively to class terms, with the usual precautions against
capture. These operations are purely syntactic. Substitution of an
arbitrary extended term for a variable will be introduced and justified
later.

The formation clause is understood stagewise. At any stage, only
predicative reductions already justified at preceding stages may be
used to admit further defining conditions. That the construction can
indeed be continued through all finite stages is a result to be proved,
not an assumption implicit in the definition.

\nbgpara{2}{Bounded Class Terms}

It is convenient to introduce bounded notation at once, without giving
it an independent formation rule.

\begin{definition}[Bounded Class Terms]
\label{def:bounded-class-terms}
Let \(S\) be an admitted extended term, let \(\xi\) be a variable not
occurring free in \(S\), and let \(\varphi\) be an extended formula.
Whenever the general class term on the right is admitted, we define
\[
\{\xi\in S\mid\varphi\}
\defequiv
\{\xi\mid(\xi\in S\land\varphi)\}.
\]
The expression on the left is called a \emph{bounded class term}.
\end{definition}

Thus bounded notation is available precisely when its unbounded
expansion has a predicative defining condition. In particular, any
class terms occurring in \(S\) or \(\varphi\) must already have been
admitted, and the condition \(\xi\in S\land\varphi\) must be
predicative according to the reductions then available.

The bound variable \(\xi\) binds its free occurrences in
\(\varphi\), but none in \(S\). The condition on \(S\) prevents the
expansion from binding an occurrence that was intended to be a
parameter. A bounded class term is always understood through the
displayed expansion; no separate existence or elimination theorem will
be needed for it.

\nbgpara{3}{Builder Rank}

A class term may contain further class terms in its defining formula.
To order their introduction, we associate a finite \emph{builder rank}
with each extended expression.

\begin{definition}[Builder Rank]
\label{def:builder-rank}
Variables and formulas containing no class terms have builder rank
\(0\). If
\[
T=\{\xi\mid\varphi\},
\]
then
\[
\operatorname{br}(T)=\operatorname{br}(\varphi)+1.
\]
The builder rank of any other extended formula is the greatest rank of
the class-term occurrences within it, or \(0\) if there are none.
Bounded class terms have the rank of their unbounded expansions. Hence
\[
\operatorname{br}\bigl(\{\xi\in S\mid\varphi\}\bigr)
=
1+\max\bigl\{\operatorname{br}(S),
             \operatorname{br}(\varphi)\bigr\}.
\]
\end{definition}

At rank \(0\), the available extended terms are variables, and the
predicative formulas are those admitted before class-builder notation.
Suppose that class terms up to rank \(n\) have been justified, together
with the reductions required to use them in predicative conditions.
A new general class term \(\{\xi\mid\varphi\}\) may be admitted if
\(\varphi\) is predicative using only those resources and has builder
rank at most \(n\). The new term then has rank at most \(n+1\).
In particular, the defining formula of a term of rank \(n+1\) contains
only class terms of rank at most \(n\).

Builder rank and predicativity have different functions. Rank keeps
track of the dependence of one class term on others; predicativity
determines which conditions Class Formation permits us to use. Rank
alone cannot make a defining condition predicative. The proof that
these two requirements can be met together will be given in the
Class-Term Extension Metatheorem.

\nbgpara{4}{Canonical Membership Conditions}

To associate a membership condition with a class term, we must take
account of the fact that only sets can belong to classes.

\begin{definition}[Canonical Membership Condition]
\label{def:technical-canonical-membership-condition}
Let
\[
T=\{\xi\mid\varphi\}
\]
be a general class term, and let \(\zeta\) be any variable. Choose
an alphabetic variant \(\varphi^*\) of \(\varphi\) in which
\(\zeta\) is free for \(\xi\). The \emph{canonical membership
condition} of \(T\), with argument \(\zeta\), is
\[
\operatorname{Cond}_T(\zeta)
\defequiv
\Set(\zeta)\land\varphi^*[\zeta/\xi].
\]
\end{definition}

Such a variant can always be obtained by renaming bound variables
within \(\varphi\) as necessary to avoid capture. Different
legitimate choices produce alphabetic variants of the resulting
condition. The notation \(\operatorname{Cond}_T(\zeta)\) is
metalinguistic: it abbreviates an extended formula and introduces no
additional symbol into the language.

The conjunct \(\Set(\zeta)\) is essential. A defining condition may
hold of a proper class, but this cannot make that proper class a
member of the class being defined. If \(T\) has rank \(n+1\), then
\(\operatorname{Cond}_T(\zeta)\) contains only class terms of rank at
most \(n\). In particular, the construction of the membership
condition does not reintroduce the outer builder.

For a bounded term, the canonical membership condition is obtained
from its unbounded expansion. Thus, if
\(T=\{\xi\in S\mid\varphi\}\), then
\[
\operatorname{Cond}_T(\zeta)
\defequiv
\Set(\zeta)\land
\bigl(\zeta\in S\land\varphi^*[\zeta/\xi]\bigr),
\]
with the same precautions against capture. We do not yet appeal to
the generalized Membership Law to simplify this expression.

\nbgpara{5}{Realizations}

We can now describe, without presupposing its existence, what a class
realizing a class term would have to satisfy.

\begin{definition}[Realization of a Class Term]
\label{def:technical-realization-of-class-term}
Let \(T\) be a general class term and \(\eta\) a variable. Choose a
variable \(\alpha\) not occurring in \(T\) and distinct from
\(\eta\). We define
\[
\Real_T(\eta)
\defequiv
\forall\alpha\bigl(
\alpha\in\eta
\leftrightarrow
\operatorname{Cond}_T(\alpha)
\bigr).
\]
For bounded class terms, \(\Real_T(\eta)\) is defined through the
corresponding general class term.
\end{definition}

Thus \(\Real_T(\eta)\) expresses that \(\eta\) has precisely the
members specified by \(T\). The symbol \(\Real\), like
\(\operatorname{Cond}\), is merely a metalinguistic abbreviation.
Different legitimate choices of \(\alpha\) yield alphabetic variants.

We have not asserted that any realization exists. That assertion will
take the form
\[
\vdash\exists\eta\,\Real_T(\eta),
\]
where \(\eta\) is chosen not to occur free in \(T\), so that the
quantifier does not bind any of its parameters. Existence will require
Class Formation, and uniqueness will follow from Extensionality.
Neither result belongs to the present syntactic preliminaries.

\nbgpara{6}{Contextual Elimination}

Since the primitive language has no non-variable terms, a class term
cannot be replaced by a primitive term in isolation. We instead
eliminate it in the formula in which it occurs, introducing a variable
to stand for a realization.

\begin{definition}[Contextual Elimination]
\label{def:contextual-elimination}
First expand all bounded class terms into general class terms. Let
\(\Phi\) be an extended formula containing a class-term occurrence.
Select the leftmost outermost occurrence \(T\), where
\emph{outermost} means that the occurrence is not within the
defining formula of another class term.

The selected occurrence lies in a unique atomic extended subformula
\(\theta\) of \(\Phi\). Choose a variable \(\eta\) not occurring in
\(\Phi\) and let \(\theta'\) result from replacing just that
occurrence of \(T\) in \(\theta\) by \(\eta\).
Replace this occurrence of \(\theta\) in \(\Phi\) by
\[
\exists\eta\bigl(
\Real_T(\eta)\land\theta'
\bigr).
\]
The resulting formula is one \emph{contextual elimination step} of
\(\Phi\). The surrounding logical context is left unchanged.
\end{definition}

For example, the respective elimination steps for
\(A\in T\), \(T\in A\), and \(A=T\), with \(A\) a variable, are
\[
\begin{aligned}
A\in T
&\quad\rightsquigarrow\quad
\exists\eta(\Real_T(\eta)\land A\in\eta),\\
T\in A
&\quad\rightsquigarrow\quad
\exists\eta(\Real_T(\eta)\land\eta\in A),\\
A=T
&\quad\rightsquigarrow\quad
\exists\eta(\Real_T(\eta)\land A=\eta).
\end{aligned}
\]
Only the selected occurrence is replaced; other occurrences of the
same term are treated separately. In particular, the procedure does
not replace a class term inside the defining formula of another
class term before eliminating that outer term. This observation will
also matter when we later justify predicative reductions.

A contextual elimination step is a prescribed expansion of notation,
not an application of an inference rule. Whether the class term has a
realization is a separate question, to be settled after the syntactic
procedure has been defined.

\nbgpara{7}{Primitive Expansion}

\begin{definition}[Primitive Expansion of a Formula Containing Class Terms]
\label{def:primitive-expansion-with-class-terms}
A \emph{primitive expansion} of an extended formula containing class
terms is obtained by repeating contextual elimination until no class
terms remain, and then expanding the remaining defined notation by
the previously assigned clauses until a formula of the primitive
language is obtained.
\end{definition}

The procedure is specified here for formulas generated by the
formation clauses already available. Defined predicates and relations
whose application to arbitrary class terms has not yet been admitted
are not thereby licensed. Their extensions, where wanted, will be
treated later.

Primitive expansion must be distinguished from predicative reduction.
Contextual elimination employs unrestricted quantifiers, both in
\(\Real_T(\eta)\) and in the existential formula introduced at each
step. It therefore establishes a way to remove class terms from
formulas, not a way to admit formulas containing them as predicative
conditions for further Class Formation instances. That second task
will require independently justified predicative reductions.

\nbgpara{8}{Syntactic Properties of Contextual Elimination}

We first settle what can be established about primitive expansion
without using any class-existence principle.

\begin{metatheorem}[Syntactic Properties of Contextual Elimination]
\label{meta:syntactic-contextual-elimination}
For every extended formula \(\Phi\) generated by the class-term
formation clauses, contextual elimination has the following
properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The procedure terminates after finitely many steps.
\item Legitimate choices of fresh variables yield results that are
alphabetic variants. Consequently, primitive expansion is determined
up to alphabetic variation.
\item Primitive expansion preserves the free variables of \(\Phi\).
\item Primitive expansion is compatible with capture-avoiding
substitution of variables for variables, in the sense required for
extended syntax in the section on Definitions.
\end{enumerate}
These claims concern the syntax alone and require neither Class
Formation nor the existence of realizations.
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
We work with general class terms, bounded terms having first been
expanded as prescribed.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Termination.}
Count all occurrences of general class builders in the complete
formula, including those within the defining formulas of other
builders. Every elimination step removes the selected outer builder.
Its defining formula appears exactly once in
\(\operatorname{Cond}_T(\alpha)\), and hence in
\(\Real_T(\eta)\). Thus the builders occurring inside \(T\) are
retained, but none is duplicated; no new builder is introduced.
The total number of builder occurrences decreases by exactly one.
Since this number is finite, contextual elimination terminates.
The remaining defined notation is eliminated by the already assigned
primitive-expansion procedures.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Alphabetic invariance.}
For a fixed selected occurrence, different choices of the fresh
realization variable and of the variables bound within its realization
condition yield alphabetic variants. The selected occurrence itself
is determined by the prescribed leftmost-outermost rule. Alphabetic
variation preserves the arrangement of class-term occurrences, so it
also preserves that selection. Induction on their number shows that
all legitimate executions of contextual elimination yield alphabetic
variants. The previously specified expansion clauses for the
remaining notation preserve this property.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Free variables.}
If \(\alpha\) and \(\eta\) are fresh for \(T\), then
\(\operatorname{Cond}_T(\alpha)\) has precisely the free variables
of \(T\), together with \(\alpha\), and
\(\Real_T(\eta)\) has precisely those of \(T\), together with
\(\eta\). In an elimination step, the free variables contributed
by the selected occurrence of \(T\) therefore reappear in its
realization condition, while the new variable \(\eta\) is bound by
the existential quantifier. No other free occurrences are altered.
Each step preserves the free variables of the complete formula, as
do the remaining primitive-expansion clauses.


\medskip
\noindent\emph{Compatibility with substitution.}
We first establish the compatibility of canonical membership
conditions by simultaneous structural induction on extended
terms and formulas.

For atomic formulas, the claim follows from the recursive
substitution clauses for their constituent terms; the
propositional cases are immediate. At quantifier and
class-builder binders, bound variables are renamed whenever
necessary to avoid capture. The induction hypothesis then
applies to the resulting bodies.

In particular, let \(T=\{\xi\mid\varphi\}\), and consider
substitution of \(\nu\) for \(\mu\). If \(\mu=\xi\), the
substitution does not enter the defining formula, since
\(\xi\) is bound by the builder. Otherwise, first rename
\(\xi\), if necessary, to avoid \(\nu\). Choosing
\(\alpha\) fresh for all expressions involved, the
substitutions of \(\alpha\) for \(\xi\) and \(\nu\) for
\(\mu\) then commute up to alphabetic variation. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Cond}_{T[\nu/\mu]}(\alpha)
 \qquad \text{and} \qquad
\bigl(\operatorname{Cond}_T(\alpha)\bigr)[\nu/\mu]
\]
are alphabetic variants.

The same property follows for \(\Real_T(\eta)\), with
fresh \(\eta\). Consequently, a contextual elimination
step commutes with variable substitution up to alphabetic
variation: substitution preserves the selected class-term
occurrence, and the corresponding realization conditions
agree up to alphabetic variation.

Induction on the number of remaining builder occurrences
extends this result to the complete elimination procedure.
The previously established substitution properties of
ordinary defined notation then yield the corresponding
claim for primitive expansion.
\end{proof}

The metatheorem establishes that class-term notation has a coherent
primitive-expansion procedure. It does not establish that realizations
exist, or that expressions containing class terms may be used as
predicative defining conditions. These are the two substantive tasks
of the Class-Term Extension Metatheorem that follows.





\subsubsection{The Class-Term Extension Metatheorem}

\nbgpara{1}{Membership Specifications}

The syntactic preliminaries have supplied a primitive-expansion procedure
for formulas containing class terms. They have not established that any
class term has a realization, or that formulas containing such terms may
be used as predicative conditions in further applications of Class
Formation. We now establish these two properties together.

It will be convenient to express the membership condition associated
with an extended term without substituting arbitrary extended terms
for variables.

Let \(T\) be an admitted extended term and \(\zeta\) a variable. We use
the following metalinguistic abbreviation:
\[
M_T(\zeta)
\defequiv
\begin{cases}
\zeta\in T, & \text{if \(T\) is a variable},\\[2mm]
\operatorname{Cond}_T(\zeta), & \text{if \(T\) is a class term}.
\end{cases}
\]
For bounded class terms, the second clause is understood through their
unbounded expansions. As usual, the alphabetic variations needed to
avoid capture are made before forming canonical membership conditions.

We shall also write
\[
\operatorname{Spec}_T(h)
\defequiv
\forall_S a\bigl(a\in h\leftrightarrow M_T(a)\bigr),
\]
where \(a\) is fresh for \(T\) and \(h\). When \(h\) is introduced
as a representative of \(T\), it is also chosen fresh for \(T\).
This too is only a metalinguistic abbreviation.

Two elementary observations will be used throughout. For every
admitted extended term \(T\),
\[
\vdash M_T(\zeta)\to\Set(\zeta).
\]
When \(T\) is a variable, this follows by Set-I; when \(T\) is a
class term, it follows from the definition of
\(\operatorname{Cond}_T\). Moreover, if \(T\) is a class term of
builder rank \(n+1\), then the class terms occurring in
\(M_T(\zeta)\) have rank at most \(n\). If \(T\) is a variable,
\(M_T(\zeta)\) contains no class terms.

Neither observation asserts the existence of realizations or the
admissibility of substituting class terms for variables. The notation
merely brings the relevant membership formulas into a common form.

\nbgpara{2}{Class-Term Extension}

Recall that a class term of rank \(n+1\) is formed from a condition
whose constituent class terms have rank at most \(n\), and whose
predicative reductions must already have been justified. The
existence of realizations and the availability of new reductions
must therefore be established in that order at each rank.

\begin{metatheorem}[Class-Term Extension]
\label{meta:class-term-extension}
The stagewise extension by general and bounded class terms is
legitimate at every finite builder rank. More precisely:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Existence and Uniqueness of Realizations.}
For every admitted class term \(T\), and every variable \(\eta\)
not occurring free in \(T\),
\[
\vdash\exists\eta\,\Real_T(\eta).
\]
Furthermore, for distinct variables \(\eta\) and \(\zeta\) for
which the realization conditions are defined,
\[
\vdash
\bigl(\Real_T(\eta)\land\Real_T(\zeta)\bigr)
\to\eta=\zeta.
\]

\item \emph{Predicative Reductions of Atomic Formulas.}
Every atomic extended formula involving at least one class term has
a justified predicative reduction to an equivalent extended formula.
Every class term occurring in its prescribed reduct has strictly
smaller builder rank than the greatest rank occurring in the
original atomic formula. The reduction introduces no free variables
and uses no unrestricted quantifiers.
\end{enumerate}
The reductions at a given rank are justified only after the
realizations required at that rank have been established; no instance
of Class Formation is justified by appeal to the very reduction whose
correctness depends upon that instance.
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
We proceed by induction on builder rank. At each stage we also use
the following consequence of the available atomic reductions: every
formula of the admitted predicative syntax whose class terms have
rank at most that stage can be reduced using those already justified
clauses. To see this, reduce outer atomic occurrences before the
class terms within them. Each atomic reduction removes at least one
builder and retains its defining formula once, so the total number
of builders decreases. The general result is stated separately after
the induction. In particular, establishing the reductions at one
rank makes them available for defining terms at the next.

At rank \(0\), extended terms are variables. Predicative formulas
and the relevant reductions have already been justified in the
preceding sections, so the initial stage is available.

Suppose that the construction has been justified through rank \(n\),
including the admissibility of predicative conditions formed using
class terms of rank at most \(n\). We establish the results at rank
\(n+1\).

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Existence and Uniqueness.}
Let
\[
T=\{\xi\mid\varphi\}
\]
be a general class term of rank \(n+1\). By its formation clause,
\(\varphi\) is a predicative extended formula whose class terms have
rank at most \(n\). Its reductions are thus justified by the
induction hypothesis.

Choose \(\alpha\) and \(\eta\) distinct and fresh for \(T\).
Capture-avoiding substitution of the fresh variable \(\alpha\) for
\(\xi\) preserves predicative admissibility: the reductions already
available for \(\varphi\) can be carried through after this
variable-for-variable substitution, with bound variables renamed as
necessary. Thus \(\varphi[\alpha/\xi]\) is predicative. This uses
only the syntactic substitution properties established earlier, not
substitution of arbitrary extended terms.

Class Formation by Predicative Reduction now yields
\[
\vdash\exists\eta\,\forall_S\alpha
\bigl(\alpha\in\eta\leftrightarrow
\varphi[\alpha/\xi]\bigr).
\]
By the equivalence between restricted and unrestricted membership
specifications, this implies
\[
\vdash\exists\eta\,\forall\alpha
\bigl(\alpha\in\eta\leftrightarrow
(\Set(\alpha)\land\varphi[\alpha/\xi])\bigr).
\]
The formula within the unrestricted specification is precisely
\(\operatorname{Cond}_T(\alpha)\). Hence
\[
\vdash\exists\eta\,\Real_T(\eta).
\]

If both \(\Real_T(\eta)\) and \(\Real_T(\zeta)\) hold, the two
realization specifications imply
\[
\forall\alpha\bigl(
\alpha\in\eta\leftrightarrow\alpha\in\zeta
\bigr).
\]
Extensionality gives \(\eta=\zeta\). Bounded class terms require
no separate argument, since they are abbreviations of general
class terms. Since \(T\) was arbitrary, existence and uniqueness
have now been established for every new term of rank \(n+1\),
before any new atomic reduction is assigned.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Membership and Identity with Variable Arguments.}
Before assigning new reductions, we establish two equivalences for
an arbitrary admitted class term \(T\) of rank at most \(n+1\). Let \(\zeta\) be any variable and
choose a realization variable \(h\) fresh for the complete formula.
By contextual elimination,
\[
\zeta\in T
\quad\defequiv\quad
\exists h\bigl(\Real_T(h)\land\zeta\in h\bigr).
\]
The realization specification entails
\(\zeta\in h\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Cond}_T(\zeta)\).
Conversely, a realization exists, and its membership specification
supplies \(\zeta\in h\) whenever
\(\operatorname{Cond}_T(\zeta)\) holds. Therefore
\[
\vdash\zeta\in T\leftrightarrow M_T(\zeta).
\tag{M}
\]
Here and below, the displayed extended formulas are understood by
their contextual expansions.

Similarly,
\[
\zeta=T
\quad\defequiv\quad
\exists h\bigl(\Real_T(h)\land\zeta=h\bigr).
\]
Identity elimination gives one direction of
\[
\vdash\zeta=T\leftrightarrow\Real_T(\zeta),
\tag{I}
\]
and identity reflexivity and existential introduction give the
other. Both equivalences use only variable-based logical rules.
They will be stated separately as the fundamental laws in the next
part of the section; they are not additional assumptions here.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Predicative Reduction: Membership with a Variable
on the left.}
Let \(\zeta\) be a variable and \(T\) a class term. By
\((\mathrm{M})\), we may prescribe
\[
\zeta\in T\quad\rightsquigarrow\quad M_T(\zeta).
\]
The equivalence is justified, and the reduct contains only class
terms of rank below that of \(T\).

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Predicative Reduction: Membership with a Class
Term on the Left.}
Let \(S\) be a class term and \(T\) any admitted extended term.
Choose \(h\) and \(a\) fresh for the complete formula. Eliminating
the occurrence of \(S\) and applying \((\mathrm{M})\) when \(T\)
is a class term gives
\[
\vdash
S\in T
\leftrightarrow
\exists h\bigl(\Real_S(h)\land M_T(h)\bigr).
\]
When \(T\) is a variable, \(M_T(h)\) is simply \(h\in T\), so the
same equivalence holds.

Since \(M_S(a)\to\Set(a)\), the restricted and unrestricted
membership specifications are equivalent:
\[
\vdash
\Real_S(h)\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}_S(h).
\]
Also \(\vdash M_T(h)\to\Set(h)\). Thus the unrestricted
existential quantifier may be restricted to sets, yielding
\[
\vdash
S\in T
\leftrightarrow
\exists_S h\bigl(
\operatorname{Spec}_S(h)\land M_T(h)
\bigr).
\]
We therefore prescribe
\[
S\in T
\quad\rightsquigarrow\quad
\exists_S h\left(
\forall_S a\bigl(a\in h\leftrightarrow M_S(a)\bigr)
\land M_T(h)
\right).
\]
The only class terms on the right are those occurring inside
\(S\) or \(T\), each of strictly lower rank than the corresponding
outer term. Both newly introduced quantifiers are restricted.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Predicative Reduction: Identity.}
Let \(S\) and \(T\) be admitted extended terms, at least one of
which is a class term, and let \(a\) be fresh for both. We establish
\[
\vdash
S=T
\leftrightarrow
\forall_S a\bigl(M_S(a)\leftrightarrow M_T(a)\bigr).
\tag{E}
\]
Suppose first that one argument is a variable, say \(S=\zeta\),
and the other is a class term \(T\). By \((\mathrm{I})\),
\[
\vdash
\zeta=T\leftrightarrow\Real_T(\zeta).
\]
The realization condition says that \(\zeta\) and \(T\) have
exactly the same members. Since \(M_T(a)\to\Set(a)\), its
unrestricted membership specification is equivalent to
\[
\forall_S a\bigl(a\in\zeta\leftrightarrow M_T(a)\bigr).
\]
This is the right-hand side of \((\mathrm{E})\), because
\(M_\zeta(a)\) is \(a\in\zeta\). The case with the class term on
the left follows by symmetry of identity, using the corresponding
contextual expansion.

Now suppose both \(S\) and \(T\) are class terms. Choose a
variable \(h\) fresh for both. Contextual elimination of the
left-hand term, followed by \((\mathrm{I})\) for the right-hand
term, yields
\[
\vdash
S=T
\leftrightarrow
\exists h\bigl(\Real_S(h)\land\Real_T(h)\bigr).
\]
If the existential formula holds, its two realization conditions
imply agreement of \(M_S(a)\) and \(M_T(a)\) for every set
\(a\), giving the right-hand side of \((\mathrm{E})\).
Conversely, suppose that restricted agreement holds. By existence
of realizations, choose \(h\) satisfying \(\Real_S(h)\).
For every set \(a\), the assumed agreement and the realization
condition for \(S\) give
\[
a\in h\leftrightarrow M_T(a).
\]
For an object that is not a set, both sides are false: membership
implies sethood, and so does \(M_T(a)\). Hence the equivalence holds
for every object, which is precisely \(\Real_T(h)\).
Existential introduction gives the displayed existential formula,
and thereby \(S=T\). This establishes \((\mathrm{E})\) without
using a general substitution principle or Class-Builder Congruence.

We accordingly prescribe
\[
S=T
\quad\rightsquigarrow\quad
\forall_S a\bigl(M_S(a)\leftrightarrow M_T(a)\bigr).
\]
Once again the reduct has only restricted quantifiers and contains
no class terms at the maximal rank of the original atomic formula.

These cases exhaust the atomic extended formulas involving class
terms: the primitive atomic relations are \(\in\) and \(=\).
The reductions introduce no free variables, since all auxiliary
variables are bound and chosen fresh. Their equivalences were
derived from realizations already established, without appeal to
extended-term substitution, Class-Builder Congruence, or any
predicative reduction whose justification depends on the present
rank.

At rank \(n+1\), the defining conditions of all newly admitted
terms used only reductions justified at ranks at most \(n\). The
new atomic reductions have now been justified and become available
for forming terms at subsequent ranks. The induction step is
complete, and the two assertions hold at every finite rank.
\end{proof}

\nbgpara{3}{Predicative Admissibility}

The preceding metatheorem supplies reductions for atomic formulas.
We must still verify that their repeated application produces
predicative reducts of complete formulas and that the process is
non-circular.

\begin{metatheorem}[Predicative Admissibility of Class-Term Formulas]
\label{meta:predicative-admissibility-of-class-term-formulas}
Every extended formula formed from the admitted predicative syntax
and admitted class terms has a justified core predicative reduct.
Repeated application of the prescribed atomic class-term reductions
terminates, introduces no free variables, and employs only
reductions available at earlier stages of the construction when
justifying the formation of a new class term.
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
Expand bounded class terms into their general forms. Reduce atomic
subformulas containing class terms, proceeding within the ordinary
logical context of the current formula. Crucially, a reduction does
not first descend into the defining formula of a class term: that
formula becomes accessible only when an outer atomic formula
containing the term has been reduced. Thus all replacements occur
under logical connectives and quantifiers, where their equivalence
is preserved by the previously established congruence principles.
No appeal to Class-Builder Congruence is required.

To see termination, count the occurrences of class builders in the
complete formula, including those within the defining formulas of
other class terms. Each of the three atomic reductions removes at
least one outer class builder. The defining condition of each
removed builder reappears exactly once in its membership condition;
therefore its inner builder occurrences are retained without
duplication. No new builder is introduced. The total number of
builder occurrences strictly decreases at each step, so the process
terminates after finitely many steps.

The resulting formula contains no class terms and uses only the
previously admitted predicative operations. Applying their already
justified reductions produces a core predicative formula. Every
intermediate replacement is justified by a derivable equivalence,
and freshness of the new bound variables prevents capture or the
introduction of free variables.

Finally, there is no circular dependence on Class Formation. The
formation of a term of rank \(n+1\) requires only reductions
already justified for its constituent terms of rank at most \(n\).
Class Formation then supplies the new realization; only afterward
are the atomic reductions involving the new term established. Thus
no reduction is used to justify the Class Formation instance on
which the proof of that reduction depends.
\end{proof}

The Class-Term Extension Metatheorem establishes the existence of
uniquely determined classes for all admitted class terms. The
Predicative Admissibility Metatheorem establishes that such terms may
occur in further predicative defining conditions, subject to the
stagewise formation clauses. Together with the syntactic results
proved in the preceding part, these facts justify the extension of
class-forming notation. They do not yet justify arbitrary
substitution of class terms for variables in deductions; that
question will be treated separately below.



\subsubsection{Fundamental Class-Builder Laws}

\nbgpara{1}{Membership and Identity}

The Class-Term Extension Metatheorem establishes that every admitted
class term has a unique realization. It also justifies the atomic
predicative reductions associated with membership and identity. We can
now extract the fundamental laws governing general class terms.
These are derived results: they do not add axioms or inference rules
to the underlying system.

\begin{metatheorem}[Membership Law for General Class Terms]
\label{meta:membership-law-for-class-terms}
Let \(T=\{\xi\mid\varphi\}\) be an admitted general class term,
and let \(\zeta\) be any variable. With the usual alphabetic
renamings to avoid capture,
\[
\vdash
\zeta\in T
\leftrightarrow
\operatorname{Cond}_T(\zeta).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\vdash
\zeta\in\{\xi\mid\varphi\}
\leftrightarrow
\bigl(\Set(\zeta)\land\varphi[\zeta/\xi]\bigr).
\]
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
This is equivalence \((\mathrm{M})\) established in the proof of
Metatheorem~\ref{meta:class-term-extension}, together with the
definition of the canonical membership condition. The substitution
on the right is capture-avoiding, as specified in the preceding part.
\end{proof}

The occurrence of \(\Set(\zeta)\) is essential. Even if a defining
condition holds of a proper class, that proper class cannot be a
member of the class determined by the condition. Thus the
Membership Law does not in general reduce to
\(\zeta\in T\leftrightarrow\varphi[\zeta/\xi]\).

The Identity Law gives the corresponding characterization of the
class itself, rather than of its members.

\begin{metatheorem}[Identity Law for General Class Terms]
\label{meta:identity-law-for-class-terms}
Let \(T=\{\xi\mid\varphi\}\) be an admitted general class term,
and let \(\zeta\) be any variable. Then
\[
\vdash
\zeta=T
\leftrightarrow
\Real_T(\zeta).
\]
In particular, choosing \(\alpha\) fresh for the displayed formula,
\[
\vdash
\zeta=\{\xi\mid\varphi\}
\leftrightarrow
\forall\alpha\left(
\alpha\in\zeta
\leftrightarrow
\bigl(\Set(\alpha)\land\varphi[\alpha/\xi]\bigr)
\right).
\]
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
This is equivalence \((\mathrm{I})\) established in the proof of
Metatheorem~\ref{meta:class-term-extension}. The displayed
specification is the definition of \(\Real_T(\zeta)\) expanded.
\end{proof}

The two laws serve different purposes. The Membership Law determines
which objects belong to the class denoted by a builder expression.
The Identity Law determines when an arbitrary class is that class.
Both are justified using the original variable-based rules,
Class Formation, and Extensionality.

We shall not yet extract rules permitting arbitrary extended terms
to be substituted into these laws. In particular, an expression such
as \(S\in\{\xi\mid\varphi\}\), with \(S\) itself a class term,
has already received an interpretation and a predicative reduction,
but the general substitution instance
\(\varphi[S/\xi]\) has not yet been justified. That will be done
in the part on extended-term substitution.

\nbgpara{2}{Class-Builder Congruence}

The identity reduction of the Class-Term Extension Metatheorem,
together with the canonical membership conditions, yields a general
criterion for equality between class-builder expressions. Their
defining conditions need agree only on sets, since proper classes
cannot be members of any class.

\begin{metatheorem}[Class-Builder Congruence]
\label{meta:class-builder-congruence}
Let \(\varphi\) and \(\psi\) be predicative extended formulas such
that \(\{\xi\mid\varphi\}\) and \(\{\xi\mid\psi\}\) are admitted
general class terms. Then
\[
\vdash
\{\xi\mid\varphi\}
=
\{\xi\mid\psi\}
\leftrightarrow
\forall_S\xi\bigl(\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi\bigr).
\]
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
T=\{\xi\mid\varphi\},
\qquad
U=\{\xi\mid\psi\},
\]
and choose \(\alpha\) fresh for both terms. By the identity
reduction established in
Metatheorem~\ref{meta:class-term-extension},
\[
\vdash
T=U
\leftrightarrow
\forall_S\alpha
\bigl(M_T(\alpha)\leftrightarrow M_U(\alpha)\bigr).
\]
By the definitions of the membership specifications, the right-hand
side is
\[
\forall_S\alpha\left(
\bigl(\Set(\alpha)\land\varphi[\alpha/\xi]\bigr)
\leftrightarrow
\bigl(\Set(\alpha)\land\psi[\alpha/\xi]\bigr)
\right).
\]
Under the restriction \(\Set(\alpha)\), the displayed
biconditional is equivalent by propositional logic to
\(\varphi[\alpha/\xi]\leftrightarrow
\psi[\alpha/\xi]\). By the ordinary congruence of restricted
universal quantification, we therefore obtain
\[
\vdash
T=U
\leftrightarrow
\forall_S\alpha
\bigl(
\varphi[\alpha/\xi]
\leftrightarrow
\psi[\alpha/\xi]
\bigr).
\]
Since \(\alpha\) is fresh, this last specification is an
alphabetic variant of
\(\forall_S\xi(\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi)\).
This proves the claimed equivalence.
\end{proof}

In particular, if the defining conditions are provably equivalent
for every set, then the corresponding class terms are provably equal:
\[
\vdash\forall_S\xi(\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vdash\{\xi\mid\varphi\}=\{\xi\mid\psi\}.
\]
The stronger premise
\(\vdash\forall\xi(\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi)\)
is sufficient as well, since it implies the restricted
specification. It is not necessary: the conditions may differ on
proper classes without determining different classes.

For bounded class terms, the corresponding laws and congruence
principles will follow through their defining expansions, once the
generalized substitution principles are available. Here we retain
the general Class-Builder Congruence theorem because it will be
needed in the proof of Realization Invariance. Nothing in the
rankwise justification of class terms depended on this congruence
result; it is a consequence of that construction, not a premise of it.




\subsubsection{Extended-Term Substitution and Admissibility}
\label{sec:extended-term-substitution}

The preceding parts have justified the introduction of class terms,
their use in predicative defining conditions, and the fundamental
Membership and Identity Laws. None of this yet licenses substituting
a class term for a variable in an arbitrary formula or inference rule.
The primitive language still has variables as its only terms, and the
logical rules were formulated for those terms alone.

We now establish that extended terms nevertheless support the ordinary
operations of substitution and identity. The essential device is to
reason with variables representing the classes determined by extended
terms. The resulting principles are derived from the original rules;
they are not additions to the deductive system.

\nbgpara{1}{Representation by Variables}

A realization relates a class term to a variable denoting the same
class. We extend that relation to variables themselves, so that the
subsequent arguments need not repeatedly distinguish the two kinds of
extended terms.

\begin{definition}[Representation of Extended Terms]
\label{def:representation-of-extended-terms}
Let \(T\) be an admitted extended term and \(\eta\) a variable. Put
\[
\operatorname{Rep}_T(\eta)
\defequiv
\begin{cases}
\eta=T, & \text{if \(T\) is a variable},\\[2mm]
\Real_T(\eta), & \text{if \(T\) is a class term}.
\end{cases}
\]
For a bounded class term, the second clause is understood through
its prescribed expansion.
\end{definition}

This is metalinguistic notation. In particular, it does not extend
\(\Real_T\) to arbitrary extended-term arguments.
For every admitted extended term \(T\), if \(\eta\) is fresh for \(T\),
\[
\vdash\exists\eta\,\operatorname{Rep}_T(\eta).
\tag{R1}
\]
Moreover, for distinct representative variables \(\eta\) and
\(\zeta\),
\[
\vdash
\bigl(
\operatorname{Rep}_T(\eta)\land
\operatorname{Rep}_T(\zeta)
\bigr)\to\eta=\zeta.
\tag{R2}
\]
For variables, these statements follow from the primitive identity
rules; for class terms, they follow from the existence and uniqueness
part of Metatheorem~\ref{meta:class-term-extension}.

\begin{metatheorem}[Representation of Atomic Formulas]
\label{meta:representation-of-atomic-formulas}
Let \(S\) and \(T\) be admitted extended terms, and let \(\eta\)
and \(\zeta\) be distinct variables fresh for both terms. Then
\[
\left\{
\operatorname{Rep}_S(\eta),
\operatorname{Rep}_T(\zeta)
\right\}
\vdash
\bigl(S\in T\leftrightarrow\eta\in\zeta\bigr)
\]
and
\[
\left\{
\operatorname{Rep}_S(\eta),
\operatorname{Rep}_T(\zeta)
\right\}
\vdash
\bigl(S=T\leftrightarrow\eta=\zeta\bigr).
\]
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
Expand the class terms in either atomic formula by contextual
elimination. The result asserts that there are variables realizing
the terms in the respective argument positions, together with the
primitive membership or identity relation between those variables.
Under the displayed representation assumptions, uniqueness of
representatives identifies the introduced variables with \(\eta\)
and \(\zeta\). The primitive identity rules then yield the indicated
relation between \(\eta\) and \(\zeta\).

Conversely, the assumed representatives witness the existential
claims introduced by contextual elimination. If an argument is
already a variable, its representation condition is simply an
identity, and the same reasoning applies. All temporary realization
variables are chosen fresh, so the existential-elimination conditions
are satisfied.
\end{proof}

In particular, \(\vdash U=U\) already follows for every admitted
extended term \(U\): choose representatives using \((\mathrm{R1})\),
identify them by \((\mathrm{R2})\), and apply the atomic
Representation Metatheorem. Thus extended-term reflexivity is
available in the next proof, independently of the general inference
rule to be extracted later.

One consequence will be useful below. \emph{Atomic replacement by
equals} is legitimate for extended terms: whenever \(S=S'\) and
\(T=T'\) hold, we may infer
\[
(S\in T\leftrightarrow S'\in T')
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(S=T\leftrightarrow S'=T').
\tag{A}
\]
Indeed, choose fresh representatives of all four terms. The
Representation Metatheorem turns the two assumed identities into
identities between their representatives. Primitive identity
elimination gives the corresponding equivalences between the atomic
formulas involving those representatives; the Representation
Metatheorem then transfers these back. Existence of representatives
allows the auxiliary variables to be eliminated. Notice that this
argument uses only primitive identity elimination, not the
extended-term identity-elimination rule that is still to be proved.

\nbgpara{2}{Basic Defined Relations with Extended-Term Arguments}

Before introducing general substitution, we extend the defined
relations needed most often in class-forming conditions. For admitted
extended terms \(S\) and \(T\), we assign the expansions
\[
\Set(T)\defequiv\exists\eta\,(T\in\eta),
\qquad
\PC(T)\defequiv\neg\Set(T),
\]
and
\[
S\subseteq T
\defequiv
\forall\xi\,(\xi\in S\to\xi\in T),
\]
where the variables introduced on the right are fresh for the
arguments. These definitions agree with the original ones for
variable arguments.

To obtain a primitive expansion, we first expand these defined
relations into formulas involving the primitive atomic relations
\(\in\) and \(=\). We then eliminate the class terms by contextual
elimination and finally expand the remaining defined notation.
This order ensures that contextual elimination is applied only
where its defining clauses are available. Permission to use the
extended relations in predicative conditions requires separate
reductions.

Recall from Part II that
\[
\operatorname{Spec}_T(\eta)
\defequiv
\forall_S\alpha
\bigl(\alpha\in\eta\leftrightarrow M_T(\alpha)\bigr),
\]
with \(\alpha\) fresh for \(T\) and \(\eta\). We first observe
that, for every extended term \(T\),
\[
\vdash
\operatorname{Rep}_T(\eta)
\leftrightarrow
\operatorname{Spec}_T(\eta).
\tag{S}
\]
If \(T\) is a class term, this is the equivalence of its restricted
and unrestricted membership specifications, using
\(M_T(\alpha)\to\Set(\alpha)\). If \(T\) is a variable,
the implication from representation to specification follows by
identity elimination; the converse follows by Extensionality, since
only sets can be members of either class.

\begin{metatheorem}[Predicative Reduction of Basic Term Relations]
\label{meta:predicative-reduction-of-term-relations}
For admitted extended terms \(S\) and \(T\), the following
reductions are justified, with all auxiliary variables chosen fresh:
\[
\vdash
\Set(T)
\leftrightarrow
\exists_S\eta\,\operatorname{Spec}_T(\eta)
\]
and
\[
\vdash
S\subseteq T
\leftrightarrow
\forall_S\alpha
\bigl(M_S(\alpha)\to M_T(\alpha)\bigr).
\]
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
For sethood, let \(\eta\) be a fresh representative of \(T\).
The Representation Metatheorem, followed by the primitive
characterization of sethood, establishes
\[
\vdash
\Set(T)
\leftrightarrow
\exists\eta\bigl(
\operatorname{Rep}_T(\eta)\land\Set(\eta)
\bigr).
\]
Existence of representatives is needed in the direction from left
to right; the other direction uses the membership equivalence under
a representative. By \((\mathrm{S})\), the right-hand side is
\(\exists_S\eta\,\operatorname{Spec}_T(\eta)\).

For subclasshood, expand its definition. The variable-argument
Membership Law from Part III, together with the variable case of
\(M\), gives
\[
\vdash
S\subseteq T
\leftrightarrow
\forall\alpha\bigl(
M_S(\alpha)\to M_T(\alpha)
\bigr).
\]
Because \(M_S(\alpha)\to\Set(\alpha)\) is derivable, the
unrestricted and restricted universal specifications are equivalent.
This yields the stated reduction.
\end{proof}

The reduction for \(\PC(T)\) is obtained by negating the reduction
for \(\Set(T)\); analogous reductions apply to the negations of
the other defined relations. The displayed reducts contain only
restricted quantifiers and membership conditions of constituent,
lower-ranked class terms. They therefore provide justified
predicative reductions, not merely equivalent primitive expansions.
Other defined relations may be extended to class-term arguments only
when corresponding formation and reduction clauses have been
justified.

\nbgpara{3}{Substitution of Extended Terms}

\begin{definition}[Extended-Term Substitution]
\label{def:extended-term-substitution}
Let \(\varphi\) be an extended formula, \(\xi\) a variable,
and \(T\) an admitted extended term. We write
\[
\varphi[T/\xi]
\]
for the result of replacing every free occurrence of \(\xi\)
in \(\varphi\) by \(T\). The operation is defined recursively
for extended formulas and extended terms, with bound variables
renamed alphabetically whenever required to prevent capture.
\end{definition}

At a quantifier or class-builder binder for \(\xi\), substitution
does not enter the bound occurrences. At any other binder, it
proceeds into the scope after the bound variable has, if necessary,
been renamed to avoid capturing a free variable of \(T\). Bounded
class terms and defined relations are treated through their
prescribed expansions. Only free occurrences of \(\xi\) in the
\emph{original} expression are replaced; occurrences within copies
of the inserted term are not subjected to the same substitution.

Substitution may increase builder rank. In particular, inserting a
class term into the condition of another class term may produce a
builder of greater rank than either expression had before
substitution. We therefore need to establish closure under this
operation, rather than assuming it from the rank definition.

\begin{metatheorem}[Preservation of Predicative Admissibility]
\label{meta:predicativity-under-term-substitution}
Let \(\varphi\) be a predicative extended formula, \(\xi\) a
variable, and \(T\) an admitted extended term. Suppose every item
of defined notation occurring in \(\varphi\) admits extended-term
arguments of the kinds produced by the substitution and has an
already justified predicative-reduction clause for them. Then the
capture-avoiding expression
\[
\varphi[T/\xi]
\]
is an admitted predicative extended formula. The same conclusion
holds for the defining conditions of class terms occurring within
\(\varphi\).
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
Proceed by simultaneous structural induction on terms and
predicative formulas, using the formation clauses rather than any
particular sequence of reductions.

Substitution into a primitive atomic formula produces an atomic
extended formula. Its predicative reduction is justified by the
Class-Term Extension Metatheorem, unless both arguments are
variables, in which case it is already core predicative. For an
admitted defined predicate, use its extended-term reduction clause.
The propositional connectives and restricted quantifiers preserve
predicativity; alphabetic renaming ensures that substitution does
not change which occurrences are bound.

Suppose the substitution enters a term
\(U=\{\zeta\mid\psi\}\). If \(\zeta=\xi\), its bound
occurrences are unaffected. Otherwise, rename \(\zeta\) fresh for
\(T\) before substitution. By the induction hypothesis,
\(\psi[T/\xi]\) is predicative. Every constituent term of that
condition is admitted and has finite builder rank. Hence the
formation clause admits
\(\{\zeta\mid\psi[T/\xi]\}\) at the next rank. The bounded
case follows from its general expansion. This completes the
simultaneous induction.
\end{proof}

This result concerns substitution into \emph{predicative} syntax.
It does not permit unrestricted quantification in a defining
condition merely because it has been hidden within a definition.
For arbitrary extended formulas, including formulas with unrestricted
quantifiers, substitution is still a syntactic operation; its
result need only be a well-formed extended formula, not a
predicative one.

\nbgpara{4}{Realization Invariance}

We can now show that an extended formula does not distinguish a
class term from a variable representing it. This is the central
justification of extended-term substitution.

\begin{metatheorem}[Realization Invariance]
\label{meta:realization-invariance}
Let \(\varphi\) be an admitted extended formula, \(\xi\) a
variable, and \(T\) an admitted class term. Choose a variable
\(\eta\) not occurring in \(\varphi\) or \(T\), and interpret
all substitutions as capture-avoiding. Then
\[
\{\Real_T(\eta)\}
\vdash
\bigl(
\varphi[T/\xi]\leftrightarrow
\varphi[\eta/\xi]
\bigr).
\tag{RI}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\vdash
\varphi[T/\xi]
\leftrightarrow
\exists\eta\bigl(
\Real_T(\eta)\land\varphi[\eta/\xi]
\bigr).
\]
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
We prove \((\mathrm{RI})\) by simultaneous structural induction on
extended terms and formulas. For a term \(U\), the accompanying
induction claim is
\[
\{\Real_T(\eta)\}\vdash
U[T/\xi]=U[\eta/\xi].
\tag{RT}
\]
The induction also permits any surrounding assumptions not
containing the fresh variables introduced in its steps.

For a variable \(U\ne\xi\), \((\mathrm{RT})\) is an instance
of identity reflexivity. If \(U=\xi\), it is precisely the
identity \(T=\eta\), which follows from \(\Real_T(\eta)\)
by the variable-argument Identity Law, symmetry, and the
contextual interpretation of the displayed equality.

Let \(U=\{\zeta\mid\psi\}\). If \(\zeta=\xi\), the
substitution does not change \(U\), so \((\mathrm{RT})\)
follows by extended-term reflexivity as justified through
representatives in Paragraph~1. (Equivalently, both sides have
the same contextual expansion.) Otherwise, choose an alphabetic
variant with \(\zeta\) fresh for \(T\), \(\eta\), and the
surrounding formulas. By the formula induction hypothesis,
\[
\{\Real_T(\eta)\}\vdash
\psi[T/\xi]\leftrightarrow\psi[\eta/\xi].
\]
Since \(\zeta\) is not free in \(\Real_T(\eta)\), restricted
universal introduction and propositional reasoning give
\[
\{\Real_T(\eta)\}\vdash
\forall_S\zeta\bigl(
\psi[T/\xi]\leftrightarrow\psi[\eta/\xi]
\bigr).
\]
Class-Builder Congruence
(Metatheorem~\ref{meta:class-builder-congruence}) then yields
\[
\{\Real_T(\eta)\}\vdash
\{\zeta\mid\psi[T/\xi]\}
=
\{\zeta\mid\psi[\eta/\xi]\}.
\]
Both terms are admitted by the preservation theorem immediately
above; thus the application of Congruence is legitimate. The case
of bounded terms is handled through their defining expansions.

For an atomic formula \(U\in V\) or \(U=V\), the term
induction hypothesis gives the identities relating the two
versions of each argument. Atomic replacement by equals,
\((\mathrm{A})\) from Paragraph~1, now yields the required
biconditional between the atomic formulas. This invokes no
extended-term identity-elimination rule. The propositional cases
follow by ordinary reasoning. At quantifiers, rename the bound
variable if necessary, use the induction hypothesis in its scope,
and then apply the corresponding quantifier congruence; the
freshness conditions ensure that no assumption is captured.
Defined notation is handled through its expansion clauses.
This completes the simultaneous induction.

For the second assertion, existence gives
\(\vdash\exists\eta\,\Real_T(\eta)\). Under an arbitrary
realization assumption, \((\mathrm{RI})\) permits replacement
of \(\varphi[T/\xi]\) by \(\varphi[\eta/\xi]\), and
conversely. Applying existential introduction and elimination,
with \(\eta\) fresh for the remaining assumptions and the
conclusion, yields the displayed unconditional equivalence.
\end{proof}

The conclusion is stronger than a mere correspondence between
primitive expansions. It gives a derivable equivalence allowing
any occurrence of a substituted class term to be handled through
a fresh variable and its realization condition. The proof uses
Class-Builder Congruence, but does not use any extended-term
substitution rule of inference.

\nbgpara{5}{Admissible Rules and Generalized Laws}

\begin{metatheorem}[Admissibility of Extended-Term Rules]
\label{meta:extended-term-rules}
Let \(S\) and \(T\) be admitted extended terms, \(\xi\) a
variable, and \(\varphi\) an admitted extended formula. Whenever
the indicated substitutions produce admitted expressions and
are capture-avoiding, the variable-based rules admit the
following extended-term instances:
\[
\frac{\forall\xi\,\varphi}{\varphi[T/\xi]}
\quad\forall\text{-E},
\qquad
\frac{\varphi[T/\xi]}{\exists\xi\,\varphi}
\quad\exists\text{-I},
\]
\[
\frac{}{T=T}
\quad =\text{-I},
\qquad
\frac{S=T\qquad\varphi[S/\xi]}
{\varphi[T/\xi]}
\quad =\text{-E}.
\]
These rules are admissible over the original variable-based
deductive system; they add no new primitive inference rules.
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
We first observe that the original variable-based rules
remain admissible when their formulas contain extended
notation. Indeed, such formulas may be replaced by their
primitive expansions. The substitution-compatibility result
of Part I ensures that variable-for-variable substitution
commutes with expansion up to alphabetic variation. The
corresponding inference can therefore be performed in the
primitive language and its result read in the extended
notation. This observation does not presuppose any of the
extended-term rules presently being justified.

If the substituted term is a variable, the assertions
follow immediately from this observation. Suppose instead
that \(T\) is a class term. Choose a variable \(\eta\)
fresh for \(T\), \(\varphi\), and all undischarged
assumptions. Existence of realizations gives
\[
\vdash\exists\eta\,\Real_T(\eta).
\]

For universal elimination, under \(\Real_T(\eta)\)
the variable-based rule yields \(\varphi[\eta/\xi]\)
from \(\forall\xi\,\varphi\). Realization Invariance
then yields \(\varphi[T/\xi]\). Existential elimination
discharges the realization assumption.

For existential introduction, begin with
\(\varphi[T/\xi]\). Existence of a realization and
Realization Invariance supply
\[
\exists\eta\bigl(
\Real_T(\eta)\land\varphi[\eta/\xi]
\bigr).
\]
Under its witness, variable-based existential introduction
applied to \(\varphi[\eta/\xi]\) gives
\(\exists\xi\,\varphi\). The auxiliary witness is
then eliminated.

For identity introduction, choose a fresh representative
variable \(\eta\) for \(T\). Under
\(\operatorname{Rep}_T(\eta)\), primitive identity
introduction yields \(\eta=\eta\). The Atomic
Representation Metatheorem then yields \(T=T\).
Existence of representatives permits the representation
assumption to be discharged.

For identity elimination, take fresh representatives
\(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) of \(S\) and \(T\).
Under their representation assumptions, the Atomic
Representation Metatheorem gives
\[
S=T\leftrightarrow\alpha=\beta.
\]

We first establish representation invariance for variable
terms. Let \(x\) be a variable, and let \(\alpha\)
be fresh. Since
\(\operatorname{Rep}_x(\alpha)\) is simply
\(\alpha=x\), we have
\[
\{\operatorname{Rep}_x(\alpha)\}
\vdash
\varphi[x/\xi]\leftrightarrow\varphi[\alpha/\xi].
\]
To justify this without extended-term identity elimination,
consider the primitive expansions of the displayed formulas.
By substitution compatibility, these are alphabetic variants
of
\[
\varphi^\circ[x/\xi]
\quad\text{and}\quad
\varphi^\circ[\alpha/\xi],
\]
respectively. Primitive identity elimination establishes
their equivalence under \(\alpha=x\). Interpreting the
extended formulas through their prescribed expansions
gives the stated result.

Together with Realization Invariance for class terms,
this establishes representation invariance for arbitrary
extended terms. Hence, under the representation assumptions,
\[
\varphi[S/\xi]\leftrightarrow\varphi[\alpha/\xi]
\]
and
\[
\varphi[T/\xi]\leftrightarrow\varphi[\beta/\xi].
\]
Given \(S=T\) and \(\varphi[S/\xi]\), we obtain
\(\alpha=\beta\) and \(\varphi[\alpha/\xi]\).
Primitive identity elimination, applied after expansion,
yields \(\varphi[\beta/\xi]\), and representation
invariance then yields \(\varphi[T/\xi]\).

Finally, existence of representatives permits the
auxiliary representation assumptions to be discharged
by existential elimination. This establishes the
extended-term identity-elimination rule.
\end{proof}

Consequently, previously derived rules may also be used
with extended terms whenever their formation requirements
are satisfied. For example, the derived Set-I rule permits
\[
\frac{S\in T}{\Set(S)}
\quad\mathrm{Set}\text{-I}
\]
with arbitrary admitted extended terms \(S\) and \(T\).
The same applies to the previously derived subclasshood
rules. All such uses abbreviate deductions involving
variables and representatives.

\begin{metatheorem}[Generalized Membership and Identity Laws]
\label{meta:generalized-class-term-laws}
Let \(T=\{\xi\mid\varphi\}\) be an admitted general class
term, and let \(S\) be an admitted extended term. Whenever
the indicated substitutions are legitimate,
\[
\vdash
S\in T
\leftrightarrow
\bigl(\Set(S)\land\varphi[S/\xi]\bigr)
\]
and, for \(\alpha\) fresh for both terms,
\[
\vdash
S=T
\leftrightarrow
\forall\alpha\bigl(
\alpha\in S
\leftrightarrow
\operatorname{Cond}_T(\alpha)
\bigr).
\]
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a variable \(\zeta\) fresh for \(S\) and \(T\).
The variable-argument Membership Law
(Metatheorem~\ref{meta:membership-law-for-class-terms})
gives
\[
\vdash
\zeta\in T
\leftrightarrow
\bigl(\Set(\zeta)\land\varphi[\zeta/\xi]\bigr).
\]
Universal introduction on \(\zeta\) is legitimate
because the formula is a theorem and \(\zeta\) is
fresh for its parameters. The admissible extended-term
universal-elimination rule then yields the stated
membership equivalence with \(S\) in place of \(\zeta\).

For identity, the atomic identity reduction from
Metatheorem~\ref{meta:class-term-extension} gives
\[
\vdash
S=T
\leftrightarrow
\forall_S\alpha
\bigl(M_S(\alpha)\leftrightarrow M_T(\alpha)\bigr).
\]
The variable-argument Membership Law identifies
\(M_S(\alpha)\) with \(\alpha\in S\), whether \(S\)
is a variable or a class term. Moreover,
\(M_T(\alpha)\) is
\(\operatorname{Cond}_T(\alpha)\) by definition.

Both membership conditions imply \(\Set(\alpha)\).
Consequently, their biconditional holds automatically
when \(\alpha\) is not a set, and the restriction
on the universal quantifier may be removed. This
establishes the second law.
\end{proof}


\nbgpara{6}{Derived Class-Builder Rules}

The four fundamental class-builder rules are extracted from the
variable-argument Membership and Identity Laws established in
Part III. Their formulation uses the canonical membership condition
and the realization condition introduced in Part I. The subsequent
justification of extended-term substitution does not change the
statement of these rules.

\begin{metatheorem}[Derived Class-Builder Rules]
\label{meta:derived-class-builder-rules}
Let \(T\) be an admitted class term and \(\zeta\) a variable.
Whenever the indicated conditions are defined, the following rules
are admissible:
\[
\frac{\zeta\in T}
{\operatorname{Cond}_T(\zeta)}
\quad\mathrm{CB}{\in}\text{-E}
\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{Cond}_T(\zeta)}
{\zeta\in T}
\quad\mathrm{CB}{\in}\text{-I}.
\]
\[
\frac{\zeta=T}
{\Real_T(\zeta)}
\quad\mathrm{CB}{=}\text{-E}
\qquad
\frac{\Real_T(\zeta)}
{\zeta=T}
\quad\mathrm{CB}{=}\text{-I}.
\]
The class-builder variable and any auxiliary bound variables are
renamed as necessary to avoid capture.
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
The Membership Law gives
\(\vdash\zeta\in T\leftrightarrow
\operatorname{Cond}_T(\zeta)\), and the Identity Law gives
\(\vdash\zeta=T\leftrightarrow\Real_T(\zeta)\).
Each rule follows by Biconditional Elimination and the established
rule-extraction principle. Hence none introduces new deductive power.
\end{proof}

The four rules are deliberately stated with a variable argument,
as required by the definitions of \(\operatorname{Cond}_T\) and
\(\Real_T\). Uses involving arbitrary extended terms are supported
by the generalized laws and admissibility results of this part;
they do not require expressions such as \(\Real_T(S)\), which have
not been defined.




\subsubsection{Bounded Class-Builder Laws and Conservativity}
\label{sec:bounded-builder-laws-and-conservativity}

The preceding parts have established the fundamental Membership and
Identity Laws, justified substitution of extended terms, and extracted
the four class-builder rules. Two matters remain. First, we record the
particularly simple laws governing bounded class terms. Second, we
make explicit why the extended notation and the rules justified for
it introduce no new deductive power.

\nbgpara{1}{Fundamental Laws for Bounded Class Terms}

Bounded class terms were introduced in Part I as abbreviations of
general class terms. They therefore require neither a new formation
principle nor a second existence proof. The generalized laws from
Part IV now give their familiar membership and identity conditions,
even when the bound and the argument are themselves class terms.

\begin{metatheorem}[Generalized Bounded Class-Builder Laws]
\label{meta:generalized-bounded-builder-laws}
Let
\[
T=\{\xi\in S\mid\varphi\}
\]
be an admitted bounded class term, where \(S\) is an admitted
extended term and \(\xi\) does not occur free in \(S\). Let \(U\)
be an admitted extended term, and suppose that the substitutions
below are legitimate. Then
\[
\vdash
U\in T
\leftrightarrow
\bigl(U\in S\land\varphi[U/\xi]\bigr).
\tag{BM}
\]
Moreover, if \(\alpha\) is a variable fresh for all the displayed
expressions, then
\[
\vdash
U=T
\leftrightarrow
\forall\alpha\bigl(
\alpha\in U
\leftrightarrow
(\alpha\in S\land\varphi[\alpha/\xi])
\bigr).
\tag{BI}
\]
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
By the definition of bounded notation,
\[
T\defequiv\{\xi\mid(\xi\in S\land\varphi)\}.
\]
The Generalized Membership Law, together with capture-avoiding
substitution, gives
\[
\vdash
U\in T
\leftrightarrow
\bigl(\Set(U)\land U\in S\land\varphi[U/\xi]\bigr).
\]
The extended-term instance of Set-I establishes
\(\vdash U\in S\to\Set(U)\). Hence the sethood conjunct is
redundant, which proves \((\mathrm{BM})\).

For identity, the Generalized Identity Law yields
\[
\vdash
U=T
\leftrightarrow
\forall\alpha\bigl(
\alpha\in U
\leftrightarrow
\bigl(\Set(\alpha)\land\alpha\in S
\land\varphi[\alpha/\xi]\bigr)
\bigr).
\]
Since \(\alpha\in S\to\Set(\alpha)\) is derivable, the same
simplification within the universal specification gives
\((\mathrm{BI})\).
\end{proof}

In particular, no explicit sethood condition is needed in the
membership law for bounded class terms: membership in the bounding
class already implies sethood. Equality between two admitted bounded
terms may likewise be characterized by applying Class-Builder
Congruence to their unbounded expansions. No additional congruence
principle is required.

\nbgpara{2}{Eliminability and Conservativity}

Syntactic termination alone does not establish conservativity.
We must also establish that primitive expansion preserves the
content of extended formulas, and that deductions employing
extended notation can be replaced by deductions in the
primitive language. The realization and admissibility results
established above provide the necessary justification.

\begin{metatheorem}[Adequacy of Primitive Expansion]
\label{meta:adequacy-of-primitive-expansion}
Let \(\Phi\) be an admitted extended formula and let
\(\Phi^\circ\) be any of its prescribed primitive expansions.
Then
\[
\vdash_{\mathrm{ext}}
\Phi\leftrightarrow\Phi^\circ.
\]
Here the equivalence is understood within the justified
extension of the original notation. In particular, the
assertion does not mean that the displayed biconditional
is itself a formula of the primitive language. Different
legitimate expansions yield alphabetically variant
primitive formulas.
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
First expand any defined relations admitting extended-term
arguments into their prescribed formulas involving the
primitive atomic relations \(\in\) and \(=\). Next perform
contextual elimination, and finally expand the remaining
defined notation.

Consider one contextual elimination step. Let \(T\) be the
selected class-term occurrence in an atomic subformula
\(\theta\), and let \(\theta'\) be obtained by replacing
that occurrence by a fresh variable \(\eta\).
Realization Invariance gives
\[
\vdash_{\mathrm{ext}}
\theta\leftrightarrow
\exists\eta\bigl(
\Real_T(\eta)\land\theta'
\bigr).
\]
This equivalence was justified using realizations and the
original variable-based rules, without presupposing any
new inference principle. Ordinary congruence for the
logical connectives and quantifiers permits the replacement
within the surrounding formula. Contextual elimination
does not descend into the defining condition of an
uneliminated class term.

Repeating this argument yields an equivalence between
\(\Phi\) and the formula obtained after all class terms
have been eliminated. The remaining expansion clauses
are justified definitions and preserve equivalence.
Termination and alphabetic invariance follow from the
Syntactic Properties Metatheorem
\ref{meta:syntactic-contextual-elimination}.
Combining the successive equivalences establishes
the assertion.
\end{proof}

The preceding metatheorem concerns equivalence within the
extended notation. Conservativity requires a further
step: the translation must preserve derivability using
only the original axioms and inference rules.

\begin{metatheorem}[Conservativity of Class-Term Notation]
\label{meta:conservativity-of-class-term-notation}
Suppose a deduction in the extended notation uses only
admitted class terms and defined relations, the original
logical and NBG axioms, justified instances of Class
Formation by Predicative Reduction, and the derived
extended-term inference rules established above.

Then the deduction can be replaced by a deduction using
only primitive formulas and the original axioms and
inference rules.

More precisely, if
\[
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm{ext}}\Phi,
\]
then, writing \(\Gamma^\circ\) for primitive expansions
of the undischarged assumptions and \(\Phi^\circ\) for
a primitive expansion of the conclusion,
\[
\Gamma^\circ\vdash_{\mathrm{NBG}}\Phi^\circ.
\]
Consequently, for every primitive formula \(\chi\),
\[
\vdash_{\mathrm{ext}}\chi
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vdash_{\mathrm{NBG}}\chi.
\]
\end{metatheorem}

\begin{proof}
We proceed by induction on the construction of an
extended deduction. More precisely, we establish
the following invariant: for every subdeduction
\(\mathcal D\) with undischarged assumptions
\(\Gamma\) and conclusion \(\Phi\), there is
a deduction in the primitive system of
\(\Phi^\circ\) from \(\Gamma^\circ\).
The translation must preserve the discharge of
assumptions and all variable conditions.

For an assumption, its primitive expansion serves
as the corresponding assumption. Primitive axioms
are unchanged. An admitted instance of Class
Formation by Predicative Reduction is replaced
by the derivation from the corresponding core
predicative instance established in the Class
Formation section. The rank induction of Part II
guarantees that the reductions used in this
derivation have already been justified.

For applications of the original logical rules,
the induction hypothesis supplies primitive
deductions of the expanded premises. The
corresponding primitive inference then yields
the expanded conclusion. In the quantifier
cases, compatibility of primitive expansion
with capture-avoiding variable substitution
ensures that the required instances agree up
to alphabetic variation. Preservation of free
variables ensures that the side conditions
of universal introduction and existential
elimination remain satisfied. Auxiliary variables
may be renamed fresh as necessary. Propositional
assumption discharge is preserved directly.

For an application of a derived extended-term
rule, we use its previously established derivation
from the original variable-based rules. Unfolding
the extended notation in that derivation supplies
a primitive deduction from the expansions of
its premises to the expansion of its conclusion.
The same applies to the four fundamental
class-builder rules, whose admissibility was
established from the Membership and Identity
Laws. Defined relations and bounded class terms
are handled through their prescribed expansions.

Thus every inference in the extended deduction
has a primitive replacement, and these
replacements can be composed while preserving
the translated undischarged assumptions.
The induction yields
\[
\Gamma^\circ\vdash_{\mathrm{NBG}}\Phi^\circ.
\]

If \(\chi\) is primitive, its expansion is
\(\chi\) itself, up to alphabetic variation.
Hence an extended proof of \(\chi\) without
assumptions yields a proof of \(\chi\) in the
original NBG system.
\end{proof}

The technical justification is now complete. Class terms
may be used as ordinary mathematical terms within the
admitted syntax, and the four fundamental class-builder
rules may be employed directly in deductions. Their
admissibility rests on the existence and uniqueness of
realizations, the justified predicative reductions, and
the representation and substitution results established
above. Neither the notation nor its associated rules
enlarges the underlying theory.
